# Adja Speech Corpus Design

Written 2026-05-05 to design a 250-hour Adja audio corpus, recorded from
scratch in the Couffo region of Benin through a Berlin-based radio contact who
can recruit speakers and arrange recording. The corpus is designed for the
thesis tracks (ASR, TTS, MT, speech translation) and as a public research
artifact for low-resource Gbe-language speech research.

This doc is the source of truth for the corpus design conversation. It pairs
with [docs/direct-s2st-feasibility-2026-05-04.md](direct-s2st-feasibility-2026-05-04.md)
(why direct speech-to-speech is not feasible without this corpus) and is the
brief I will hand to my Berlin contact and to Rolando before any speakers are
recruited.

## Constraints I am designing against

Three real-world constraints shape every decision below.

1. **Adja text scarcity.** Adja is overwhelmingly an oral language. There is no
   Adja Wikipedia at scale, no Adja newspapers, no large Adja literature
   corpus in machine-readable form. The text inventory I have access to is
   roughly 20,000 sentences total, drawn from `data/extra_adja_text.txt`,
   Tatoeba parallel pairs, GATITOS, the [Neurosymbolic AI paper](https://github.com/JosueG/neurosymbolic-ai-paper-experiments)
   parallel set, and the Adja New Testament. That is most of what exists.
   Read-aloud cannot be the foundation of the corpus, because we run out of
   text in week three.
2. **Transcriber labor pool.** The world population of viable Adja
   transcribers (fluent in Adja, literate in Adja with consistent
   orthography, willing to do this work for weeks) is in the tens, not the
   hundreds. Realistic recruitment: 5–10 trained transcribers through SIL
   Bible translation networks, the Couffo translator team from the
   Neurosymbolic paper, Gbe-language linguists at the University of
   Abomey-Calavi, and Rolando's collaborators. The corpus design has to fit
   what this team can actually transcribe in 12 weeks.
3. **No archive access.** The radio station's archives are not accessible to
   us. Every hour of audio is recorded fresh, which means we pay full cost
   per hour and we cannot lean on existing broadcast diversity. The corpus
   has to be designed entirely from scratch, with all variety coming from
   protocol design rather than archive sampling.

## The corpus shape, in one table

Five recording protocols ("buckets"), each designed to produce something
specific for the thesis and the field. Total target is 250 hours.

| Bucket | Hours | Speakers | Recording protocol | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Curated sentence reading | 20h | ~40 speakers, 30 min each | Studio, single mic, sentence prompts on screen | Phonetic anchor for ASR, free transcripts (prompt = text) |
| 2. Storytelling and monologue on prompts | 80h | ~40 speakers, ~2h each | Studio, single mic, open-ended prompts ("tell me a folk tale," "describe the market") | Natural-prosody ASR data, oral-narrative cultural artifact, transcribed text for MT |
| 3. Spontaneous dialog | 60h | ~25 speaker pairs, ~2h per pair | Studio, two close-mics, topic prompts but unscripted | Conversational ASR, code-switching corpus, speaker diarization data |
| 4. TTS-quality voices | 30h | 2 speakers, 15h each | Studio with serious acoustic treatment, single voice per session, neutral register | Working Adja TTS (the failure mode of Track 4 fixed) |
| 5. Parallel Adja-French speech | 40h | ~15 speakers, ~2h each | Studio, three sub-protocols (read aligned text, interpret in real time, retell own story in French) | Direct S2TT, evaluation set for cascade |
| 6. Domain coverage (clinic, school) | 20h | ~15 speakers | Studio, scripts written with a clinician and a teacher | Application-specific vocabulary for thesis Ch. 7 use cases |
| **Total** | **250h** | **~95–110 unique speakers** | | |

These are recording targets. The transcription targets are different and are
laid out below.

## The tiered transcription plan

The most important design decision in this corpus is that **it is not
uniformly transcribed**. Three tiers:

- **Tier 1 (Gold):** full human transcription, two-pass QA, NFC-normalized,
  agreed orthography. Used for held-out test sets and the main labeled
  training corpus. This is what the thesis evaluates against.
- **Tier 2 (Silver):** first-pass transcript auto-generated by current XLS-R
  (or Whisper-Ewe) ASR, then human transcriber post-edits rather than typing
  from blank. Used for training data where 5–10% transcript noise is fine.
- **Tier 3 (Unlabeled):** raw audio plus speaker metadata, no transcript.
  Released for self-supervised learning, codec training, and continued
  pretraining of multilingual speech foundation models. **This is itself a
  research contribution.**

Allocation across the buckets:

| Bucket | Hours | Tier 1 (Gold) | Tier 2 (Silver) | Tier 3 (Unlabeled) | Transcriber-hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Read-aloud | 20h | 20h (free, prompts) | 0 | 0 | ~30 (verification only) |
| 2. Storytelling | 80h | 30h | 0 | 50h | ~90 (post-edit) |
| 3. Dialog | 60h | 20h | 0 | 40h | ~80 (post-edit, two channels) |
| 4. TTS voices | 30h | 20h (free, prompts) | 10h (post-edit) | 0 | ~50 |
| 5. Parallel speech | 40h | 15h | 15h | 10h | ~110 (incl. French translation) |
| 6. Domain coverage | 20h | 0 | 20h | 0 | ~60 |
| **Total** | **250h** | **105h** | **45h** | **100h** | **~420 hrs** |

A team of 6 trained Adja transcribers, working 6 hours a day, 5 days a week,
for 12 weeks, has a capacity of ~2,160 transcriber-hours. The 420-hour need
fits comfortably with margin for QA passes and rework.

## Why this design is also a stronger thesis

The methodology paragraph reads as:

> *"I collected 250 hours of Adja speech across five recording protocols.
> One hundred and five hours were gold-transcribed and serve as the labeled
> training and evaluation core. Forty-five hours were transcribed using an
> ASR-bootstrapped post-edit workflow and serve as silver-tier training data.
> The remaining 100 hours are released as an unlabeled Adja audio corpus
> suitable for self-supervised learning, codec training, and continued
> pretraining of multilingual speech foundation models. This tiered design
> reflects the practical reality of working in a language with a small
> literate transcription labor pool, and the unlabeled subset is itself a
> research contribution, since no public Adja audio at this scale exists in
> any prior corpus."*

That is more honest, more replicable, and more interesting than a paragraph
that claims uniform gold transcription of 250 hours.

## Source text strategy

The 105 gold-transcribed hours need source material. Where it comes from, by
bucket:

- **Bucket 1 (read-aloud, 20h).** Curated from existing inventory: ~10k
  cleaned unique sentences from `data/extra_adja_text.txt` and Tatoeba pairs,
  ~5h of Adja New Testament passages where licensing allows, and ~5h of
  newly-translated French source sentences (paid translation of Tatoeba
  French, simple Wikipedia leads, health pamphlets) which produces parallel
  text as a side benefit.
- **Bucket 2 (storytelling, 80h).** No prior text needed. Speakers generate
  the speech. The "text inventory" is roughly 100–150 prompts designed by me
  and Rolando in advance, organized by domain (folk narrative, daily life,
  agriculture, food, religion, trade, family, education, health, travel).
- **Bucket 3 (dialog, 60h).** Same as Bucket 2: prompts, not scripts. About
  40 conversational topic prompts.
- **Bucket 4 (TTS, 30h).** Drawn from Bucket 1's curated sentence list, with
  filler sentences if needed. The TTS speakers re-read these sentences in
  controlled studio conditions.
- **Bucket 5 (parallel, 40h).** Method A (15h) reuses existing Tatoeba and
  Neurosymbolic-paper Adja-French pairs. Method B (15h) interprets new French
  source content. Method C (10h) has Bucket 2 storytellers retell their own
  story in French.
- **Bucket 6 (domain, 20h).** Scripts written with a clinician and a teacher,
  domain-specific. About 1,500–2,000 sentences.

## Speaker recruitment requirements

Demographic targets, in writing, before any contracts:

- **~95–110 unique speakers** across the corpus. Some appear in multiple
  buckets (e.g., a Bucket 2 storyteller may also do Bucket 5 Method C).
- **Gender balance:** 50% women, 50% men.
- **Age bands:** roughly 30% under 30, 40% 30–50, 30% over 50.
- **Dialect register:** balanced across the major Couffo subdialect zones,
  with the recording engineer asked to log dialect on every session.
- **TTS voices (Bucket 4):** 2 dedicated speakers, one woman and one man,
  willing to commit 15 hours each over 4–6 weeks of sessions. Paid more than
  the rest. Contracts include a re-record clause.
- **Conversation pairs (Bucket 3):** 25 pairs, recruited as pairs (friends,
  family members, market colleagues) so the conversation has natural rapport.
- **Bilingual speakers (Bucket 5):** 15 fluent French-Adja bilinguals for the
  parallel-speech bucket.

Speaker consent (in Adja and French) covers research use, derivative model
training, and HuggingFace publication of audio plus transcripts. Use the
[Common Voice consent template](https://commonvoice.mozilla.org/) or the
[Masakhane data ethics template](https://www.masakhane.io/) as a starting
point rather than writing one from scratch.

## Transcriber recruitment requirements

This is the harder recruitment problem. Recruit in parallel with speakers,
not after.

- **6–8 trained Adja transcribers**, available 6 hours a day for 12 weeks
  starting in week 3.
- **Recruitment networks:** SIL Adja Bible translation team, Couffo
  translator team from the Neurosymbolic paper, University of Abomey-Calavi
  Gbe linguists, religious workers in Adja literacy programs, teachers from
  any Adja-language program. Rolando's network is the lead channel here, not
  the Berlin contact's.
- **Training:** one-page orthography protocol document plus a half-day
  workshop with example transcriptions. The protocol covers NFC
  normalization, tone-marking convention (consistent yes or no, pick one
  answer and stick to it), no spelling-correction by transcribers, treatment
  of code-switched French (transcribe French in French, Adja in Adja),
  treatment of interjections and false starts, segmentation rules, and
  timestamp granularity.
- **Tooling:** [Label Studio](https://labelstud.io/), [ELAN](https://archive.mpi.nl/tla/elan),
  or a custom Praat-based workflow. Audio comes in, gets fed to current XLS-R
  for first-pass auto-transcript, lands in the tool, transcriber post-edits.
  Two-pass QA (a second transcriber reviews 100% of Tier 1, samples Tier 2).
- **Pay structure:** per-hour pay rather than per-utterance, so transcribers
  do not rush at the expense of quality.

## Pilot phase, non-negotiable

Before any full-scale recording: a **10-hour pilot** that exercises every
bucket in miniature.

- 2 speakers × 1h Bucket 1
- 1 speaker × 2h Bucket 2
- 1 speaker pair × 1h Bucket 3
- 1 TTS voice × 2h Bucket 4
- 2 speakers × 1h Bucket 5 (one each Method A and Method B)
- 1 clinician scripted × 1h Bucket 6

Run the full transcription pipeline (auto-transcript, post-edit, QA passes)
on the pilot. Then sit with the result and answer five questions:

1. Does the orthography protocol survive contact with real speakers and
   transcribers, or do we get inconsistencies we did not anticipate?
2. Are the prompts producing the diversity we wanted, or are speakers all
   answering the same way?
3. Is the audio acoustically clean enough at 48 kHz mono to feed XLS-R and
   Whisper without retraining?
4. Did the post-edit ratio land at 3x audio length as estimated, or is it
   2x or 5x? This number reshapes the timeline.
5. Are the ethics, consent, and metadata workflows tight enough to scale to
   100 speakers without losing track of what was recorded by whom and under
   what consent terms?

The pilot will reveal at least three things the design got wrong. That is
the entire point. Build a 2-week pilot phase into the project plan and do
not skip it under deadline pressure.

## Timeline (in weeks, target 14–16 weeks total)

| Weeks | Phase | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Design and recruitment | Prompts written, orthography protocol finalized, Bucket 1 sentence list curated, speakers recruited, transcribers recruited and trained, ethics approved |
| 3–5 | Pilot recording and review | 10h pilot recorded, transcribed end-to-end, protocol revised based on findings |
| 5–11 | Full-scale recording | All 250h recorded across buckets, in parallel where speaker pools allow |
| 6–13 | Rolling transcription | Transcription starts the moment the pilot is approved and runs in parallel with recording |
| 13–14 | QA and packaging | NFC normalization passes, metadata cleanup, splits, HuggingFace upload prep |
| 14–16 | Buffer | Re-records, missing data, contingency |

## Per-track impact for the thesis

What the thesis can claim, per chapter, with this corpus in hand:

- **ASR (Ch. 4).** Fine-tune XLS-R, Whisper, MMS, and Omni-ASR variants on
  100h gold-labeled Adja. Continued pretraining on the unlabeled 100h to
  produce an Adja-adapted XLS-R checkpoint. Per-character error analysis on
  tonal phonology becomes statistically meaningful at this scale.
- **TTS (Ch. 5).** Fine-tune Sesame CSM, Orpheus, Qwen3-TTS, and MMS-TTS on
  20–30 hours of single-voice studio data. Track 4's "converged loss but
  unintelligible audio" failure mode is fixed by the data scaling alone.
- **MT (Ch. 3).** Add ~100h-worth of newly-transcribed Adja text to the
  monolingual side. The 75-hour parallel speech subset's transcripts feed
  Adja-French parallel text. Domain coverage from Bucket 6 closes the
  clinic-and-school vocabulary gap.
- **Pipeline (Ch. 6).** End-to-end Adja voice interface with components that
  individually work, evaluated on Bucket 5's parallel speech as the test set.
- **Direct S2ST (Ch. 7 future-work-becomes-real).** With 30h gold paired
  Adja-French speech, attempt a **direct** speech-to-speech baseline (unit-
  based or Translatotron-class), using the data-budget discussion in
  [docs/direct-s2st-feasibility-2026-05-04.md](direct-s2st-feasibility-2026-05-04.md)
  as the planning reference. The "what would unlock direct S2ST" paragraph
  in `07_discussion_sections/03_future.tex` becomes "I ran the experiment."

## Per-track impact for the field

What the corpus contributes to research outside this thesis:

- **First public Adja speech corpus at scale.** Currently zero public Adja
  hours exist on HuggingFace, FLEURS, MMS, or any benchmark.
- **First Adja-pretrained SSL checkpoint.** XLS-R or MMS continued
  pretraining on 100h unlabeled Adja audio produces a model that downstream
  researchers can fine-tune for ASR with much less labeled data.
- **First Adja-French parallel speech set.** 15h Method A pairs are
  release-grade.
- **Code-switching corpus.** 60h dialog with code-switching is a publishable
  resource separate from the speech-recognition work.
- **Adja oral-narrative cultural archive.** 80h of Couffo storytelling is a
  permanent contribution to UNESCO endangered-language work, Glottolog, and
  Adja-language preservation.

## Open questions that depend on the contact's answer

Before any contracts move, three questions:

1. **Recruitment scale.** Can the contact recruit ~95–110 unique speakers in
   Couffo, balanced for gender and age, including 2 dedicated voice talent
   willing to commit 15 hours each? If the answer is "I can recruit 30
   speakers," the corpus shape changes.
2. **Studio capacity.** Where is recording happening, and is the studio
   acoustically suitable for Bucket 4 TTS-grade work? If the only available
   studio is a radio recording booth shared with daily broadcasting, Bucket
   4's quality target needs a reality check.
3. **Transcriber recruitment.** Can the Berlin contact help recruit Adja
   transcribers, or does that responsibility fall entirely to Rolando's
   network? If the latter, recruitment lead time is longer and the project
   should not commit to an aggressive timeline.

The first message to the contact should be a scope conversation, not a
procurement order: "Here is what I am designing, here are the protocols and
the speaker mix and the transcription burden, what is feasible to recruit
and record in Couffo before we commit to numbers?"

## What to send the contact in the first email

A two-page version of this doc, plus three explicit asks:

1. Confirm or revise the speaker recruitment scale (95–110 speakers across
   the demographic targets above).
2. Confirm studio suitability for Bucket 4 (TTS-grade single-voice 15-hour
   recording, with consistent acoustic environment across 4–6 weeks of
   sessions).
3. Confirm whether his network can help recruit transcribers, and if so,
   how many and on what timeline.

Everything else (sentence curation, prompt design, orthography protocol,
ethics, transcriber training, recording schedule) is internal to me and
Rolando, and does not need to be in the first message to the contact.
